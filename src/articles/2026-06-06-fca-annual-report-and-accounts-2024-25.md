## Background

The Annual Report and Accounts is a primary accountability document for Parliament, regulated firms, and the public on the FCA's stewardship of the UK financial services regulatory framework. It reports against the FCA's 2022–2025 three-year strategy, which is built around 13 enduring commitments grouped under three aims: reducing harm, raising standards, and promoting competition and positive change.

The report is published as the FCA concludes its 2022–2025 strategy and prepares to launch its 2025–2030 strategy. This follows a period of legislative change, including the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023, the introduction of the Consumer Duty, APP fraud mandatory reimbursement, and the Smarter Regulatory Framework replacing retained EU law.

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 introduced a secondary objective for the FCA to facilitate international competitiveness and growth. The SICGO reporting reflects this objective, which forms part of the Edinburgh and Mansion House reform programme.

## Developments

The FCA published its Annual Report and Accounts for the financial year 2024/25 on 6 June 2026. The report sets out progress against the FCA's 2022–2025 three-year strategy and its 13 enduring commitments.

The report includes a dedicated section on Commitment 1, reducing and preventing financial crime, and Commitment 2, putting consumers' needs first, consistent with the FCA's Consumer Duty and financial crime priorities. Commitment 3 covers strengthening the UK's position in global wholesale markets, reflecting the Edinburgh and Mansion House reform agenda and the secondary international competitiveness and growth objective.

The publication includes governance disclosures, financial statements, outcomes and metrics, and a suite of related reports. A separate SICGO report and SICGO metrics document were published alongside the Annual Report, demonstrating the FCA's accountability against its secondary objective. HM Treasury is the recipient of the SICGO report. Related reports published alongside the Annual Report include diversity, equity and inclusion progress, the FCA's response to the Complaints Commissioner's Annual Report 2024/25, climate-related financial disclosure, a modern slavery statement, pay gap data, and operating service metrics including enforcement data.

The report affects FCA regulated firms across all sectors, including authorised payment institutions and e-money institutions, consumer credit firms, claims management companies, mutual societies, and primary and wholesale market participants, as well as consumers of UK financial services. The FCA's Annual Public Meeting for 2025 was held on 9 October 2025, with responses to unanswered questions published as part of the accountability package.
