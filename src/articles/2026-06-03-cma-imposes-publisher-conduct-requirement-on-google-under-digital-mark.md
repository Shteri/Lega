## Key Takeaways

1. On 3 June 2026, the CMA imposed a publisher conduct requirement on Google in respect of its general search and search advertising services.
2. This is the first conduct requirement imposed on Google after the CMA designated the firm as having Strategic Market Status (SMS) in general search services.
3. The requirement follows a consultation that the CMA launched on 28 January 2026 on four proposed conduct requirements.
4. The publisher conduct requirement is intended to secure fairer treatment for publishers in Google's search services.
5. It forms part of a broader package of proposed conduct requirements covering data portability, fair ranking, and user choice.

## Background

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCCA) created the Strategic Market Status (SMS) regime. Under this regime, the CMA can designate a firm as having SMS and impose conduct requirements on it. The DMCCA came into force in January 2025. The CMA's Digital Markets Unit administers these powers.

The CMA opened its SMS investigation into Google on 14 January 2025, when it published an Invitation to Comment. This document set out the scope of the investigation, the main avenues of investigation, and potential issues and interventions. The Invitation to Comment closed on 3 February 2025.

On 24 June 2025, the CMA published its proposed SMS decision and a roadmap for possible interventions in respect of Google's general search services. The roadmap included potential conduct requirements and pro-competition interventions. That consultation closed on 22 July 2025. On 10 October 2025, the CMA published its final decision to designate Google as having SMS in general search services, comprising its general search and search advertising activities.

## Developments

On 28 January 2026, the CMA launched a consultation on four proposed conduct requirements: Data Portability, Publisher, Fair Ranking, and User Choice. In February 2026, the CMA hosted online roundtables for affected stakeholders. These included interested businesses, trade associations and civil society bodies, and covered each of the proposed conduct requirements. The CMA published responses to the consultation on 18 March 2026.

On 3 June 2026, the CMA imposed the publisher conduct requirement on Google in respect of its general search and search advertising services. This is the first conduct requirement imposed on Google following the SMS final decision of 10 October 2025. The requirement is intended to secure fairer treatment for publishers in Google's search services.

The entities affected include Google, which is the SMS-designated firm subject to the conduct requirement, and online publishers, which are the direct beneficiaries. Search advertising customers are also affected.

The CMA published a publisher conduct requirement document (a 4.7MB PDF), a final decision report (a 2.7MB PDF), and a consumer survey report (a 2.8MB PDF).

## What to Watch

The publisher conduct requirement is one of four proposed conduct requirements set out in the January 2026 consultation. The other three cover data portability, fair ranking, and user choice. The CMA's roadmap published on 24 June 2025 also referred to potential pro-competition interventions in respect of Google's general search services.
