## Key Takeaways

1. On 28 May 2024, OFSI reissued General Licence INT/2024/4761108 under Regulation 64 of the Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019.
2. The licence allows a Person to use the retail banking services of a designated Credit or Financial Institution, provided that payments made or received are intended for the personal use of a Person.
3. On 23 February 2026, the licence was amended and extended.
4. On 26 May 2026, the licence was updated to add a new definition of 'Cryptoasset' and a new reporting requirement.
5. The new reporting requirement, set out in paragraph 5A, applies to anyone using cryptoassets to make or receive a payment under the licence.
6. The licence is published jointly by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) and HM Treasury.

## Background

The licence sits within the UK's autonomous sanctions regime against Russia. This regime is administered by OFSI under HM Treasury. The licence operates under Regulation 64 of the Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, which forms part of the OFSI financial sanctions licensing framework.

The licence provides a route for personal remittances that involve designated financial institutions subject to Russia sanctions. It applies to designated Credit or Financial Institutions, and to Persons (natural or legal) seeking to make or receive personal remittances involving those institutions.

## Developments

On 28 May 2024, OFSI reissued General Licence INT/2024/4761108. The licence permits a Person to make use of the retail banking services of a designated Credit or Financial Institution. Payments made or received under the licence must be intended for the personal use of a Person.

The licence has since been amended twice. On 23 February 2026, the licence was amended and extended.

On 26 May 2026, the licence was updated to introduce a new definition of 'Cryptoasset'. The definition reads: 'A cryptographically secured digital representation of value or contractual rights that uses a form of distributed ledger technology and can be transferred, stored or traded electronically.' The same update introduced a new reporting requirement at paragraph 5A. This requirement applies to anyone using cryptoassets to make or receive a payment under the licence.

The changes affect designated Credit or Financial Institutions, Persons seeking to make or receive personal remittances involving those institutions, and Persons using cryptoassets to make or receive payments under the licence. They also affect UK financial services firms handling Russia-related payments, and the compliance and legal teams at banks and payment institutions dealing with Russia sanctions.

The General Licence is published as a 4-page PDF of 118 KB. An accompanying Publication Notice is a 3-page PDF of 290 KB.

## What to Watch

Persons using cryptoassets to make or receive payments under the licence are now subject to the reporting requirement in paragraph 5A. Affected firms and individuals should review the updated licence terms that took effect on 26 May 2026.
