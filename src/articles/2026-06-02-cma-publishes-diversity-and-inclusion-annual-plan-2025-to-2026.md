## Key Takeaways

1. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) published its Diversity and Inclusion Annual Plan for 2025 to 2026 on 28 March 2025.
2. An updated version of the Annual Plan was published on 2 June 2026.
3. The Annual Plan forms part of the CMA's wider Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) Strategy, which covers the period 2025 to 2028.
4. The plan sets out the CMA's road map for building and supporting a diverse and inclusive workforce.
5. The new strategy supersedes the CMA's previous diversity and inclusion strategy, which covered the period 2020 to 2024.
6. This is an internal HR and organisational governance document and does not contain regulatory or competition law enforcement content.

## Background

The CMA is the UK's competition regulator. As a public sector employer, it is subject to the Public Sector Equality Duty under the Equality Act 2010.

The CMA developed its Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Strategy for 2025 to 2028 over the course of 2025. This strategy replaces the CMA's previous diversity and inclusion strategy, which covered the period 2020 to 2024.

The Annual Plan is an internal workforce and organisational governance publication. It does not relate to the CMA's regulatory, competition enforcement, or digital markets functions.

## Developments

The CMA published its Diversity and Inclusion Annual Plan 2025 to 2026 on 28 March 2025. A further update to the Annual Plan was published on 2 June 2026.

The Annual Plan sits within the CMA's broader EDI Strategy for 2025 to 2028. It sets out the CMA's road map for delivering its aim to continue to build and support a diverse and inclusive workforce.

The document applies to the CMA's workforce and staff. It reflects the CMA's position as a public sector employer subject to the Public Sector Equality Duty under the Equality Act 2010.

## What to Watch

The Annual Plan covers the period 2025 to 2026 and sits within the EDI Strategy running to 2028. The 2 June 2026 update indicates that the CMA continues to revise the plan over its term.
