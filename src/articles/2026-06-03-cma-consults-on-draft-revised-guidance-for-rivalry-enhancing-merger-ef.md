## Key Takeaways

1. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has launched a public consultation on draft revised guidance covering its approach to assessing rivalry-enhancing efficiencies in mergers.
2. The draft guidance is intended to replace paragraphs 8.2 to 8.27 of the existing Merger Assessment Guidelines.
3. The review into the CMA's approach to rivalry-enhancing efficiencies was launched in January 2026 as part of the CMA's 4Ps programme, which covers pace, predictability, proportionality, and process.
4. The CMA has published three documents alongside the consultation: the draft revised guidance (10 pages), a consultation document summarising key proposed changes (29 pages), and responses to the prior call for evidence.
5. The consultation closes at 5pm on 1 July 2026.
6. The consultation is being held on an external website rather than directly on GOV.UK.

## Background

The consultation forms part of the CMA's reform of its merger control regime. The CMA has been implementing a broader programme known as the 4Ps since early 2026. The four points are pace, predictability, proportionality, and process. The aim is to make merger review faster, more predictable, proportionate, and process-driven.

The revised efficiencies guidance is one part of that reform. The existing Merger Assessment Guidelines have been criticised for setting an overly high bar for efficiency claims. The CMA's merger control work operates within the framework set by the Enterprise Act 2002.

The review into the CMA's approach to rivalry-enhancing efficiencies in mergers was launched in January 2026.

## Developments

The CMA is consulting on draft revised guidance that is intended to replace the text currently in paragraphs 8.2 to 8.27 of the Merger Assessment Guidelines on rivalry-enhancing efficiencies.

The consultation was published and opened for responses on 3 June 2026. Alongside the consultation, the CMA has published three documents: the draft revised guidance, which runs to 10 pages (175 KB); a consultation document summarising key proposed changes, which runs to 29 pages (363 KB); and responses to an earlier call for evidence.

The changes affect merging parties seeking to rely on efficiency arguments in CMA merger reviews, legal and economic advisers working in UK merger control, and businesses subject to CMA Phase 1 and Phase 2 merger investigations.

The consultation is being held on an external website rather than directly on GOV.UK.

## What to Watch

The consultation closes at 5pm on 1 July 2026. Interested parties can submit responses until that deadline.
