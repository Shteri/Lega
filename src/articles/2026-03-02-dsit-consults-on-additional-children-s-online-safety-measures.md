## Key Takeaways

1. The Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) ran a national consultation on children's online safety from 10:30am on 2 March 2026 to 11:59pm on 26 May 2026.
2. The consultation sought views on potential minimum age restrictions for social media, gaming sites and AI chatbots, restrictions on addictive design features, and improved parental support.
3. The consultation considered raising the digital age of consent and how age verification and age assurance technologies can support implementation.
4. Three surveys were offered: a full consultation for civil society, industry and the public; a survey for parents and carers of young people aged 21 and under; and a survey for children and young people aged 10 to 21.
5. On 16 February 2026, the Prime Minister announced new legal powers to allow the government to act after the consultation response without waiting for new primary legislation.
6. On 4 June 2026, DSIT published a preliminary analysis of responses to one consultation question and a summary of the total number of responses received.
7. The government committed to publishing its full response in summer 2026.

## Background

This consultation sits within the post-Online Safety Act 2023 phase of UK children's online safety policy. In this phase, the government is examining whether additional legislative measures are needed beyond Ofcom's codes of practice and the existing duties of care framework to address the impact of technology on children's wellbeing.

The consultation built on the foundation of the Online Safety Act 2023 and examined whether further steps are needed to ensure the UK remains the safest place for children online. The government stated that 'social media use among children and adolescents is almost universal' and that 'the proportion of children with social media profiles has increased significantly in the last 5 years.'

The announcement of new legal powers to act without fresh primary legislation points to potential secondary legislation or executive action to implement consultation outcomes.

## Developments

DSIT launched the consultation at 10:30am on 2 March 2026 and closed it at 11:59pm on 26 May 2026. The consultation was described as 'the next step in the government's work to ensure children's experiences online are safe and enriching.' The consultation document ran to 76 pages (ISBN 978-1-5286-6278-9).

The consultation considered setting a minimum age for children to access social media, restricting risky functionalities and design features such as infinite scrolling and autoplay, whether the digital age of consent should be raised, and how age verification and age assurance technologies can support effective implementation. It also considered whether the guidance on the use of mobile phones in schools should be put on a statutory footing, and better support for parents including clearer guidance and simpler parental controls.

Three separate surveys were offered: a full consultation for civil society, industry and members of the public; a survey for parents and carers of young people aged 21 and under; and a survey for children and young people aged 10 to 21.

On 16 February 2026, the Prime Minister announced new legal powers to allow the government to act swiftly after the consultation response, without waiting for new primary legislation.

On 28 May 2026, the Secretary of State for Science, Innovation and Technology referenced figures from this consultation in a media interview. This prompted DSIT to publish a preliminary analysis of responses to one consultation question and a summary of the total number of responses received. That analysis was published on 4 June 2026.

The measures under consideration affect children and young people aged 10 to 21, parents and carers, social media platforms, gaming sites, AI chatbot providers, schools, civil society organisations and industry stakeholders.

## What to Watch

The government committed to publishing its response to the consultation in summer 2026. The new legal powers announced on 16 February 2026 would allow the government to act on the consultation findings without waiting for new primary legislation.
