## Key Takeaways

1. HM Treasury has published a Keep Well Agreement with the National Wealth Fund (NWF), under which HM Treasury, as sole Shareholder, commits to ensuring the NWF has sufficient funds to meet its payment obligations.
2. The version published on 28 May 2026 is described as an unexecuted version, released for transparency and publication purposes.
3. HM Treasury and the National Wealth Fund jointly published the document as a policy paper under the topic of government efficiency, transparency and accountability.
4. The Keep Well Agreement is a 7-page PDF document of 125 KB and is not described as a statutory instrument or primary legislation.
5. The agreement is a bilateral financial commitment between HM Treasury and the NWF.
6. Requests for accessible formats of the document can be directed to digital.communications@hmtreasury.gov.uk.

## Background

The National Wealth Fund was established as part of the UK government's industrial strategy and clean energy investment agenda. It builds on and rebrands the UK Infrastructure Bank.

A Keep Well Agreement is a mechanism used in public finance. It provides a liquidity assurance backed by government without creating a formal full guarantee on the government's balance sheet.

The NWF acts as a channelling vehicle for strategic public investment within the broader framework of the UK government's industrial and investment strategy.

## Developments

On 28 May 2026, HM Treasury and the National Wealth Fund jointly published the Keep Well Agreement as a policy paper. The topic given for the publication is government efficiency, transparency and accountability.

Under the agreement, HM Treasury, as sole Shareholder of the National Wealth Fund, commits to ensuring the NWF has sufficient funds to meet its payment obligations.

The published document is explicitly described as an unexecuted version, released for transparency and publication purposes. It is a 7-page PDF and has a file size of 125 KB. It is not described as a statutory instrument or primary legislation, but as a bilateral financial commitment between HM Treasury and the NWF.

The parties affected include the National Wealth Fund, HM Treasury, and the counterparties and creditors of the National Wealth Fund that rely on its payment obligations.

Requests for the document in an accessible format can be directed to digital.communications@hmtreasury.gov.uk.
