1. On 15 January 2026, the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) launched a call for evidence on its approach to assessing rivalry-enhancing efficiencies in merger cases.
2. The call for evidence opened at 10am on 15 January 2026 and closed at 5pm on 26 February 2026.
3. The review forms part of the CMA's 4Ps framework programme, which covers pace, predictability, proportionality, and process.

6. The efficiencies review follows updated guidance on jurisdiction and procedure published in October 2025 and updated guidance on merger remedies published in December 2025.

## Background

The CMA assesses mergers under the framework set out in the Enterprise Act 2002. Its Merger Assessment Guidelines explain how it carries out these assessments, including how it considers efficiency claims made by merging parties.

This review sits within the CMA's programme to update its merger control approach under the 4Ps framework. The 4Ps stand for pace, predictability, proportionality, and process. The CMA frames the work as part of its commitment to support growth, investment, and business confidence across the UK economy.

The efficiencies review is the next step in this programme. In October 2025, the CMA published updated guidance on jurisdiction and procedure to clarify its approach to jurisdiction and global deals. In December 2025, it published updated guidance on merger remedies. This review is not directly linked to the digital markets SMS regime.

## Developments

The CMA opened its call for evidence at 10am on 15 January 2026. The document ran to 11 pages and 176 KB. The call for evidence invited views on the CMA's approach to assessing rivalry-enhancing efficiencies in mergers.

The CMA sought views on two specific areas. The first was its analytical approach to rivalry-enhancing efficiencies. This included the types of evidence the CMA considers and how it assesses dynamic efficiencies. The second was its process for assessing those efficiencies, including how it engages with merging parties on efficiency claims.

The call for evidence closed at 5pm on 26 February 2026. Respondents included economic consultancies such as AlixPartners, BRG, Compass Lexecon, Frontier Economics, and Econic Partners. Law firms responded, including Clifford Chance LLP, Freshfields LLP, and the City of London Law Society. Academic and policy bodies also responded, including the Centre for Competition Policy and the Balanced Economy Project.

Following the call for evidence, the CMA published a consultation on draft revised guidance on rivalry-enhancing efficiencies. The consultation page was last updated on 3 June 2026. The draft revised guidance is intended to replace the text currently in paragraphs 8.2 to 8.27 of the CMA's Merger Assessment Guidelines.

The review affects merging parties that seek to rely on efficiency claims before the CMA, as well as competition law practitioners and advisers.
