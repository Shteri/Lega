## Key Takeaways

1. HM Treasury updated the Orange Book collection on 3 June 2026 to reflect a refresh of the Central Government's Assurance Directory (CGAD).
2. The Orange Book, titled 'Management of Risk: Principles and Concepts', sets out the principles and concepts of risk management for UK government organisations.
3. The guidance is non-statutory and is intended to be read alongside the Green Book, which covers appraisal and evaluation.
4. The Orange Book is supported by several annexes covering portfolio risk management, risk appetite, risk reporting, and risk management skills.
5. The document provides the overarching risk governance framework within which cyber and operational resilience risks are managed in UK central government, though it is not a cybersecurity-specific instrument.
6. A November 2024 update to the Risk Control Framework 'bank of questions' added a new section covering UK Sanctions and extensions to Orange Book risk questions.

## Background

The Orange Book is the central UK government guidance document on risk management. It was first published in May 2013 and was substantially revised in 2023. HM Treasury issues it under its government finance and accountability remit, rather than as a cybersecurity-specific regulatory instrument.

The guidance establishes the concept of risk management and provides a basic introduction to its concepts, development, and the implementation of risk management processes in government organisations. It states that it 'should be read and used in conjunction with the other publications such as the Green Book which provides specific advice on appraisal and evaluation.'

The Orange Book sits at the apex of UK central government risk governance. It provides the foundational framework within which departments are expected to manage all categories of risk, including cyber and information security risks. It applies to UK central government departments, government agencies and public bodies, the Government Finance Function, and senior accountable officers and risk owners in HM Government organisations.

## Developments

On 3 June 2026, the Central Government's Assurance Directory (CGAD) was updated. This is the latest revision of the Orange Book collection. The CGAD Spring 2026 Excel file is 1.44 MB.

This followed earlier changes to the assurance materials. On 3 June 2025, a Quick Guide to the CGAD was added. The Quick Guide PDF is 1 MB and runs to 12 pages. On 24 April 2025, the former Risk Management bank of questions was replaced with the CGAD.

On 21 November 2024, the Risk Control Framework 'bank of questions' tool was updated. The update incorporated extensions to Orange Book risk questions, a new section covering UK Sanctions, updated references, and minor format changes.

The Orange Book itself is a 64-page PDF of 462 KB. It is accompanied by several supplementary publications: the Portfolio Risk Management Guidance Orange Book Annex (1.91 MB, 24 pages), published alongside the new edition of the Orange Book on 4 May 2023; the Risk Appetite Guidance Note version 2.0 (722 KB, 22 pages), which replaced the earlier version on 23 August 2021; the Risk Management Skills and Capabilities Framework (3.37 MB, 22 pages), added on 9 August 2021; and the Good Practice Guide: Risk Reporting (637 KB, 20 pages), added on 16 August 2021.

## What to Watch

The CGAD remains subject to periodic updates, the most recent being the 3 June 2026 revision. Affected organisations should monitor further revisions to the directory and its supporting Quick Guide.
