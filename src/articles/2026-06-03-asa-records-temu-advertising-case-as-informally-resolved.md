## Background

The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) administers the UK Code of Non-broadcast Advertising and Direct & Promotional Marketing, known as the CAP Code. This is a self-regulatory system. It sits outside the Online Safety Act 2023 and the DMCCA 2024 regulatory frameworks.

The ASA can resolve some matters informally. In an informal resolution, the advertiser agrees to amend or withdraw the advertisement in question. The ASA does not conduct a full investigation or publish a binding adjudication. These cases are noted on the public record, but they carry less detail than upheld rulings.

Temu has been subject to broader regulatory scrutiny across multiple UK and EU jurisdictions. This makes even informally resolved ASA cases relevant when tracking the platform's compliance position.

## Developments

On 3 June 2026, the ASA published its weekly rulings list. The list included Whaleco UK Ltd, trading as Temu, as one of seven informally resolved cases.

The Temu case attracted a single complaint. This was consistent with the other six informally resolved cases published on the same date. The case was resolved without a formal ruling, meaning Temu agreed to amend or withdraw the advertising in question without the ASA conducting a full investigation.

The document does not disclose the nature of the advertisement, the media channel, the topic, or the specific CAP Code rules engaged for the Temu entry.

The ASA published three formal upheld rulings on the same date. None concerned Temu. They involved Brighton & Hove City Council, regarding a poster; DUSK (Retail) Ltd, trading as DUSK, regarding savings claims on a website; and Wowcher Ltd, trading as Wowcher, regarding promotion fairness.
