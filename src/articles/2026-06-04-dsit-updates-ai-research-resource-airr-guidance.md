## Key Takeaways

1. The AI Research Resource (AIRR) is a suite of advanced supercomputers providing AI-specialised compute capacity to researchers, academia, and industry in the UK.
2. AIRR is hosted on two supercomputers: Isambard-AI at the University of Bristol and Dawn at the University of Cambridge.
3. AIRR is operated through a partnership between the UK government, UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), the University of Cambridge, the University of Bristol, HPE, Nvidia, Intel, and Dell.
4. Access is available through two primary routes: a Gateway route for researchers from academia, industry, or other UK organisations, and a Rapid Access route for UK-registered micro, small and medium organisations.
5. The guidance was first published on 17 July 2025.
6. On 7 November 2025, the AIRR Innovator route opened for applications and the corresponding guidance was published.
7. On 4 June 2026, the guidance was updated to include the AI open access call and guidance for distributed peer review applicants and reviewers.

## Background

AIRR sits within the UK government's strategy to build sovereign AI compute infrastructure as part of its pro-innovation approach to AI development. It supports the research and commercial ecosystem identified in the AI Regulation White Paper of March 2023 and subsequent National AI Strategy commitments.

Providing publicly accessible supercomputing resource is intended to widen access to frontier AI capabilities and reduce barriers for SMEs and academia. AIRR is intended to enable SMEs and the wider research and development community to drive innovation and advancement in AI.

## Developments

AIRR is a suite of advanced supercomputers offering AI-specialised compute capacity to researchers, academia, and industry. The resource is hosted on Isambard-AI at the University of Bristol and Dawn at the University of Cambridge.

It is a partnership between the UK government, UKRI, the University of Cambridge, the University of Bristol, HPE, Nvidia, Intel, and Dell. AIRR offers two primary access routes. The Gateway route is designed for researchers from academia, industry, or other UK organisations. The Rapid Access route is open to UK-registered micro, small and medium organisations.

The guidance was first published on 17 July 2025. On 7 November 2025, the AIRR Innovator route opened for applications and its guidance was published. On 4 June 2026, the guidance was updated to include the AI open access call and guidance for distributed peer review applicants and reviewers.

## What to Watch

The AI open access call and the distributed peer review process, added on 4 June 2026, are the most recent additions to the AIRR guidance. Academic researchers, industry researchers, UK-registered SMEs, and other UK organisations seeking AI compute access should review the updated applicant and reviewer guidance.
