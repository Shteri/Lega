## Background

The UK government's work on children's online safety follows the Online Safety Act 2023, which sets out age assurance requirements and child safety duties for user-to-user services. Ofcom is the online safety regulator responsible for implementing age assurance under that Act.

The prospect of a minimum age of 16 for social media access would be a potential intervention beyond the existing Online Safety Act 2023 framework. The 'Growing up in the online world' consultation gathered responses intended to inform this policy direction.

## Developments

On 28 May 2026, the Secretary of State for Science, Innovation and Technology referenced figures from the 'Growing up in the online world' consultation in a media interview. The figures concerned parental support for a minimum age of 16 for social media access.

On 1 June 2026, DSIT published an ad hoc statistical release to make the underlying data and methodology publicly available. DSIT described the release as a measure to support transparency around the figures cited publicly.

The release is described as preliminary analysis of consultation responses from the 'Growing up in the online world' consultation, categorised under the topic of online safety. The document as published contains only metadata and framing text, and does not include the detailed quantitative findings.

Parties affected by the broader policy direction include social media platforms operating in the UK, and parents and children who would be subject to any minimum age requirements. DSIT is the publishing department, and Ofcom is the regulator responsible for implementing age assurance under the Online Safety Act 2023.

## What to Watch

The release relates to ongoing government policy development on children's online safety and potential age-based restrictions on social media platforms. The consultation data is intended to inform that policy direction.
