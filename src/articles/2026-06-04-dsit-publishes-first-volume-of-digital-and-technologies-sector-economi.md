## Key Takeaways

1. On 4 June 2026, the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) published Volume 1 of its Digital and Technologies (D&T) Sector Statistics, covering economic estimates for the UK D&T sector in 2024.
2. The statistics cover company count, productivity, employment, turnover, gross value added (GVA), and wages.
3. The D&T sector is defined using a company-level definition rather than Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes.
4. The sector is defined as a combination of six frontier technology priority areas plus a digital component.
5. The six frontier technologies are Advanced Connectivity Technologies, Artificial Intelligence, Cyber Security, Engineering Biology, Quantum Technologies, and Semiconductors.
6. The publication provides figures for the D&T sector and for frontier technologies as a whole, but not for individual frontier technologies or the digital component separately.

## Background

DSIT is the central policy department for AI and digital technology in the UK. Its work supports the UK's Modern Industrial Strategy and the Digital and Technologies Sector Plan that sits within it.

The six frontier technology priority areas are identified in the Digital and Technologies Sector Plan. They are Advanced Connectivity Technologies, Artificial Intelligence, Cyber Security, Engineering Biology, Quantum Technologies, and Semiconductors.

This release provides an economic measurement framework for these prioritised technology sectors. It establishes an official statistical basis for measuring the economic scale of AI and the other frontier technology sectors in the UK.

## Developments

DSIT published Volume 1 of its Digital and Technologies Sector Statistics on 4 June 2026. The publication provides economic estimates for the UK D&T sector for the reference year 2024. It covers company count, productivity, employment, turnover, gross value added (GVA), and wages.

The D&T sector is defined as a combination of frontier technologies and a digital component. This definition is based on a company-level definition rather than SIC codes. The frontier technologies element comprises the six priority technology areas set out in the Digital and Technologies Sector Plan.

The publication presents statistics for the D&T sector and for frontier technologies as a whole. It does not present statistics for individual frontier technologies or for the digital component separately. DSIT states this is due to overlaps between the underlying company lists.

The digital component definition used in this publication differs from the Digital sector definition used in the existing Digital Sector Economic Estimates series. Statistics on individual frontier technologies are available in separate DSIT sector studies, which are linked on the definitions page of this release.

The release is accompanied by an ODS data file of 7.91 KB. The publication is made available under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

## What to Watch

This release is described as Volume 1, which indicates further volumes may follow. Readers seeking figures for individual frontier technologies can consult the separate DSIT sector studies linked on the definitions page of this release.
