## Key Takeaways

1. The FCA has published Policy Statement PS26/5, finalising new rules and guidance for short selling in the UK and implementing changes introduced by the Short Selling Regulations 2025 (SSR 2025).
2. A new Short Selling Sourcebook is being created within the FCA Handbook, largely replicating existing provisions from the current regime.
3. The deadline for reporting net short positions is extended to 23.59 T+1, and firms must keep records of their covering arrangements for five years.
4. From 13 July 2026, existing exemptions for market making in UK sovereign debt will no longer apply, and the FCA will begin publishing aggregate net short positions by company.
5. The market maker exemption notification process is being streamlined to become activity-based, replacing repeat notifications with an annual attestation.
6. The FCA retains emergency powers to prohibit, restrict, and impose additional requirements on short selling in exceptional circumstances, with a high bar for their use set out in a new Statement of Policy.

## Background

PS26/5 forms part of the UK's Smarter Regulatory Framework, which replaces retained EU law with rules tailored to UK market conditions. In short selling, this means moving away from the EU Short Selling Regulation, as onshored, towards a bespoke UK regime.

The changes sit within the broader Edinburgh and Mansion House reform agenda. That agenda aims to make UK wholesale markets more competitive and reduce regulatory friction while preserving market integrity. The legislative basis for the new regime is the Short Selling Regulations 2025 (SSR 2025), made under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023.

PS26/5 responds to feedback received on consultation paper CP25/29: Changes to the UK Short Selling Regime, which was open from 28 October 2025 to 16 December 2025.

## Developments

The FCA published PS26/5 on 16 April 2026, alongside the final short selling rules and the Statement of Policy on emergency powers. The rules create a new Short Selling Sourcebook within the FCA Handbook, largely replicating existing provisions from the current regime.

The deadline for reporting net short positions is extended to 23.59 T+1. Firms are required to keep records of their covering arrangements for five years.

A new reportable shares list will replace the list of exempt shares. It will identify shares admitted to trading on UK trading venues that are subject to the short selling rules. For issuers with multiple share classes, the list will identify the main class of ordinary shares for reporting purposes.

The market maker exemption notification process is being streamlined to become activity-based. The requirement to send further notifications to add financial instruments is removed. Market makers will instead provide an annual attestation, and transitional provisions require renotification of existing exemptions.

UK sovereign credit default swaps (CDS) are placed outside the scope of position reporting and covering requirements. From 13 July 2026, existing exemptions for market making in UK sovereign debt will no longer apply, though emergency powers will continue to cover these instruments.

From 13 July 2026, the FCA will publish aggregate net short positions (ANSPs) by company. The publication aggregates individual net short positions reported at or above the 0.2% threshold without identifying individual position holders.

The FCA retains emergency powers to prohibit, restrict, and impose additional requirements on short selling in exceptional circumstances. The new Statement of Policy sets out a high bar for their use.

The changes affect anyone conducting short selling activity, market makers in UK equities and sovereign debt, investment firms holding or managing net short positions in UK shares or sovereign debt, issuers of shares admitted to trading on UK trading venues, and UK trading venues.

## What to Watch

Two changes take effect on 13 July 2026: existing exemptions for market making in UK sovereign debt cease to apply, and the FCA begins publishing aggregate net short positions by company. Firms relying on existing market maker exemptions should note the transitional provisions requiring renotification.
