## Background

This initiative sits within the government's wider data-sharing and public-sector data reform work. That work includes the development of the National Data Library under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, which seeks to create a single trusted gateway of curated, high-quality public-sector data.

At present, health visiting, education and childcare services often operate in isolation and rely on paper-based processes. This means children's needs can be missed and support can arrive late.

Any processing of children's personal data must operate within UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. The announcement emphasises robust safeguards and strict data protection standards.

## Developments

On 4 June 2026, DSIT and the Department for Education launched a project to explore the safe and responsible connection of health, education and childcare data. The project begins with Leeds City Council, the London Borough of Hammersmith & Fulham, and councils in the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority.

The project aims to help ensure children are ready for school. Nearly a third of children (32%) are starting school without the basic skills they need. This rises to almost half (48%) of children eligible for free school meals.

The project will gather data from local authorities, education practitioners and health visitors. This data could inform a new collection on the National Data Library (NDL). The NDL currently holds only non-personal, aggregated data, such as traffic levels.

More detailed data will only be securely and ethically shared with necessary services. This will be underpinned by robust safeguards and strict data protection standards. The services affected include health visitors, education practitioners, GPs, speech and language therapists, and childcare providers.

Parents and carers are being asked to share their experiences of accessing early learning support. They are also being asked to identify where better information sharing between public sector organisations could have helped.

The project is linked to the rollout of Best Start Family Hubs. Up to 1,000 of these hubs are set to be operating by 2028, backed by almost £1 billion of investment. Free stay and play sessions are intended to save families up to £200 a year.
