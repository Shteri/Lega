## Key Takeaways

1. On 5 June 2026, the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) published a statistical report on UK participation in the EU's Framework Programmes for research and innovation.
2. The report covers Horizon 2020 from 2014 to 2020 and the first four years of Horizon Europe from 2021 to 2024.
3. The analysis draws on European Commission data and examines UK funding, participation, relative performance across programme pillars, organisation types and regions, and UK collaborations in 2024.
4. The publication is an ad hoc statistical release and does not set out new regulatory guidance.
5. The full report is 54 pages and is accompanied by an executive summary in HTML and data tables in ODS format, all available on GOV.UK.

## Background

The report sits within the UK's science and innovation policy landscape. It provides evidence of UK engagement with EU research funding frameworks following Brexit and the UK's subsequent association to Horizon Europe.

The publication is not AI-specific regulation. It is relevant to the research infrastructure and international collaboration that underpins UK AI development policy. It provides consolidated official statistics on UK engagement with EU research programmes across the period covered.

## Developments

DSIT published the report, titled "Statistics on UK Participation in EU Framework Programmes (Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe), 2014–2024", on 5 June 2026 as part of its ad hoc statistical releases collection.

The report covers Horizon 2020 from 2014 to 2020 and the first four years of Horizon Europe from 2021 to 2024. It draws on European Commission data.

The analysis examines overall trends in UK funding and participation. It includes the UK's relative performance across programme pillars, organisation types and regions, and UK collaborations in 2024.

The full PDF report is 54 pages and 3.39 MB in size. It is accompanied by an executive summary in HTML and data tables in OpenDocument (ODS) format. The data tables file is 43.8 KB. All files are available on GOV.UK.

The entities affected include UK research institutions and universities, UK businesses engaged in Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe projects, DSIT, and UK Government policymakers on science and innovation funding. The European Commission is the data source.
