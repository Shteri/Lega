## Background

This monitoring update forms part of the CMA's ongoing surveillance of the road fuel retail market. It follows the CMA's 2023 market study, which identified weak price competition and led to the introduction of the statutory Fuel Finder price transparency scheme.

The CMA carries out market study and monitoring work under the Enterprise Act 2002, alongside its powers under the Competition Act 1998. The report sits within the CMA's wider consumer-facing markets enforcement work and precedes a more formal reassessment of retailer pricing strategies.

## Developments

The CMA has published its latest road fuel monitoring report. The report covers the period to the end of April 2026 and assesses how the conflict in the Middle East has affected pump prices and retailer margins.

The CMA found no evidence that retailers actively changed pricing strategies to exploit the conflict. It concluded that elevated wholesale prices explained most of the increase in pump prices in March and into April 2026.

Average fuel margins for both supermarket and non-supermarket retailers remained at historically high levels throughout the period. Individual retailer margins increased slightly in April, bringing the average to 11.3 pence per litre, even as inventory levels and wholesale costs stabilised.

The CMA remains concerned that these sustained high margins reflect a continuation of the weak competitive dynamics identified in its 2023 market study. It said retailers continued to follow largely passive pricing policies, aligning to local market pricing set by competitors rather than actively competing to win customers.

Supermarkets remained, on average, the cheapest places to buy fuel and led the market on price. Motorway service stations were the most expensive and charged a substantial premium. The CMA noted that its Fuel Finder scheme can deliver savings of up to £9 per tank for drivers who shop around using navigation apps and comparison websites.

## What to Watch

As it is now three years since the CMA's original market study, the CMA will engage directly with retailers to conduct a more detailed assessment of pricing strategies. The results are expected to be published in autumn 2026.

The CMA will publish its next monitoring update in August 2026. That update will cover market developments to the end of June 2026 and assess whether supply improvements are being passed on to drivers.
