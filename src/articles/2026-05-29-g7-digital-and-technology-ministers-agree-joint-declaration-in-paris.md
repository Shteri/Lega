## Key Takeaways

1. G7 Digital and Technology Ministers agreed a joint ministerial declaration on 29 May 2026 in Paris, France.
2. The declaration covers four themes: AI safety, AI adoption, digital resilience and efficiency, and children's online safety.
3. The UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) published the declaration on 1 June 2026.
4. The declaration is a political commitment and does not create binding legal obligations.
5. The declaration reflects the policy direction of G7 member governments on AI governance.

## Background

The G7 is a group of leading economies whose members are Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States. The European Union also participates in G7 meetings.

G7 ministers have previously issued statements on AI. The Hiroshima AI Process, agreed by the G7 in 2023, produced principles that fed into later discussions on the governance of general-purpose AI (GPAI).

This declaration comes as major AI regulatory frameworks are entering their main enforcement phases. The EU AI Act's high-risk system obligations are entering into force in August 2026, and its GPAI rules have applied since August 2025.

## Developments

G7 Digital and Technology Ministers met in Paris on 29 May 2026 and agreed a joint ministerial declaration. The declaration sets out G7 commitments across four areas: AI safety, AI adoption, digital resilience and efficiency, and children's online safety.

DSIT published the declaration on 1 June 2026.

The declaration is a G7 political commitment. It does not itself create binding legal obligations. It reflects shared priorities among G7 member governments and the EU institutions that take part in G7 meetings.

Those affected include G7 member governments, EU institutions, AI developers and deployers operating across G7 jurisdictions, children's online safety stakeholders, and the digital infrastructure and technology sector.

## What to Watch

The EU AI Act's high-risk system obligations are entering into force in August 2026. Earlier G7 statements on AI have informed EU and national regulatory approaches, including the Hiroshima AI Process principles that fed into GPAI governance discussions.
