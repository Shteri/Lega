## Key Takeaways

1. The FTC has listed the Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (COPPA Rule) as an active rule in its Legal Library, in an entry published on 22 April 2025.
2. The COPPA Rule implements the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and governs the online collection of personal information from children under the age of 13.
3. The FTC's rules index classifies the COPPA Rule under the topic category Privacy and Security, Children's Privacy.
4. The source document is the FTC's rules browse page, which aggregates rules addressing unfair methods of competition and consumer-protective obligations across specific industries.
5. The listing confirms the rule's active status but does not establish any new operative effective date.
6. Andrew N. Ferguson is identified as the FTC Chairman at the document's publication date of 22 April 2025.

## Background

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), at 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506, and its implementing rule are among the oldest and most enforced federal sectoral privacy laws in the United States. They predate comprehensive state privacy laws by more than two decades.

The COPPA Rule is codified at 16 C.F.R. Part 312. The FTC has periodically updated it, most recently finalising amendments in January 2025 under the prior administration. The agency continues to enforce the rule alongside state children's privacy statutes, such as California's AADC and similar laws.

The FTC describes its rulemaking function as creating and enforcing rules "to address unfair methods of competition and to carry out the laws that protect consumers from deceptive and unfair business practices in particular industries, such as telemarketing."

## Developments

On 22 April 2025, the FTC listed the COPPA Rule as an active rule in its Legal Library rules index. The listing places the rule under the Consumer Protection, Children's Privacy topic category, and within the index's Privacy and Security, Children's Privacy classification.

The COPPA Rule governs the online collection of personal information from children under the age of 13 by operators of websites and online services. The age threshold below which COPPA protections apply is 13.

The entities affected by the rule include operators of websites and online services directed to children under 13, operators of general-audience websites with actual knowledge that they collect personal information from children under 13, third-party service providers acting as operators under COPPA, and FTC-regulated businesses collecting children's personal information online.

The underlying document is the FTC's rules browse page (a rules index) rather than a standalone rulemaking notice. As such, substantive regulatory detail in the source text is limited. The listing does not establish any new operative effective date.

## What to Watch

The FTC finalised amendments to the COPPA Rule in January 2025. Readers should monitor the agency's enforcement and guidance activity, as well as the continued development of state children's privacy statutes that operate alongside the federal rule.
