## Key Takeaways

1. On 3 June 2026, Chancellor Rachel Reeves delivered a speech at the Scientific Superpower Conference on the Oxford to Cambridge Growth Corridor and the Northern Growth Corridor.
2. The government is establishing a Development Corporation for Greater Cambridge as a delivery vehicle with powers and governance to accelerate development.
3. Reeves reaffirmed the Oxford to Cambridge Growth Corridor as a central part of the government's growth strategy, a commitment she first made eighteen months before this speech.
4. AstraZeneca has recommitted £300 million to its UK operations, including £200 million at the Cambridge Biomedical Campus.
5. The Ellison Institute of Technology is establishing a new global science campus in the corridor, as part of a £10 billion UK investment.
6. The corridor has £143 billion in regional Gross Value Added, with thirty percent of jobs in knowledge-intensive sectors.

## Background

This speech sits within the UK government's pro-innovation economic strategy. That strategy treats AI as a priority sector and uses regional growth corridors as a tool for development.

In her Mais lecture in March 2026, Reeves set out three 'big choices' for secure economic growth. Two of those were AI and innovation, and regional growth. The strategy positions AI as central to national economic and security planning, consistent with the emphasis on AI as a transformative technology that requires coordinated public and private investment.

The Chancellor first committed to the Oxford to Cambridge Growth Corridor eighteen months before this speech, in a speech on the outskirts of Oxford. The 3 June 2026 speech reaffirmed that commitment.

## Developments

Reeves delivered the speech on 3 June 2026. She set out the innovation credentials of the corridor and announced specific investment and infrastructure measures.

In the 2026 Global Innovation Index, Cambridge is ranked first in Europe and second globally. Oxford is ranked second in Europe and fifth globally. The corridor is ranked second in the world for mean start-up deal size among global tech clusters. Between 2011 and 2025, Oxford and Cambridge created more than 400 university spin-outs, the highest of any academic institutions in the UK.

The corridor has £143 billion in regional Gross Value Added. Thirty percent of jobs there are in knowledge-intensive sectors, which is nearly triple the national average. Employment growth in the corridor runs forty percent faster than the rest of the country.

The Chancellor cited several companies as corridor exemplars. These include Arm in Cambridge, Oxford Nanopore, CMR Surgical in Cambridge and Ely, and Aiimi in Milton Keynes.

Reeves announced that the government is establishing a Development Corporation for Greater Cambridge. This will act as a delivery vehicle with powers and governance to accelerate development. She also pointed to private investment in the corridor, including AstraZeneca's £300 million recommitment to its UK operations, of which £200 million is for the Cambridge Biomedical Campus, and the Ellison Institute of Technology's new global science campus, which forms part of a £10 billion UK investment.

## What to Watch

The Development Corporation for Greater Cambridge was announced on 3 June 2026 and is forthcoming. Its powers and governance arrangements are intended to accelerate development in the corridor. A Supercluster Board is also among the entities involved in the strategy.
